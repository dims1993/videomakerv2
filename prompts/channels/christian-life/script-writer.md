# Christian Life — Script Writer

## Purpose

This file defines how to write scripts for the Christian Life YouTube channel.

The Script Writer transforms a strong biblical angle into a complete narration that is:

- emotionally engaging
- biblically grounded
- visually easy to stage
- simple to understand
- useful for real life
- warm and hopeful
- retention-focused without manipulation

The channel does not create academic sermons.

The channel creates illustrated biblical life stories that help people understand why biblical principles matter in daily life.

The script should feel like:

**a gentle story that brings light to a real human struggle.**

---

## Core Script Formula

Every script should be built from this formula:

```text
HUMAN PAIN → WRONG RESPONSE → CONSEQUENCE → BIBLICAL LIGHT → SMALL CHOICE → PRACTICAL TRANSFORMATION → HOPEFUL CLOSE
```

The script should not begin with abstract theology.

It should begin with a real feeling.

The viewer should first recognize themselves.

Then the biblical principle should bring clarity.

# Voice Identity

The narration should sound:

- calm
- warm
- direct
- sincere
- emotionally intelligent
- clear
- reflective
- hopeful
- practical
- compassionate

The narrator should feel like someone helping the viewer see something important.

The voice should not sound:

- aggressive
- superior
- manipulative
- academic
- overly religious
- theatrical
- prosperity-focused
- fear-based
- politically charged
- internet-drama driven

---

# Viewer Relationship

Speak to the viewer as someone who may be:

- tired
- hurt
- ashamed
- confused
- anxious
- angry
- lonely
- spiritually curious
- trying to change
- wanting guidance
- needing hope

The script should make the viewer feel:

```text
I am seen.
I am not being attacked.
I am being gently challenged.
There is a better path.
There is still light.
```

---

# Language Rules

Use simple language.

Prefer:

- short sentences
- concrete images
- emotional clarity
- everyday situations
- strong metaphors
- plain biblical wisdom
- practical application

Avoid:

- complicated theology terms
- long doctrinal explanations
- church-insider phrases
- vague motivational language
- artificial drama
- exaggerated claims
- shame-heavy language
- fear-based manipulation
- prosperity promises

---

# Biblical Framing Rules

Use Scripture respectfully.

Biblical principles should be:

- faithful to the meaning
- simple to understand
- connected to daily life
- emotionally relevant
- practical
- not twisted into generic self-help
- not used as decoration

Good framing:

```text
The Bible does not teach forgiveness because the wound was small.
It teaches forgiveness because bitterness can keep the wound alive.
```

Good framing:

```text
Biblical wisdom does not always show the whole road.
Sometimes it gives enough light for the next step.
```

Bad framing:

```text
If you forgive, everything will immediately be fixed.
```

Bad framing:

```text
If you had real faith, you would not feel fear.
```

Bad framing:

```text
God will make you successful if you just follow this one principle.
```

# Scripture Usage

The script may use direct Scripture references, but it does not need to quote many verses.

Use Scripture in one of these ways:

## 1. Principle-Based

Explain the biblical idea in plain language.

Example:

```text
The Bible teaches that a soft answer can turn away wrath, because not every reaction deserves to become a fire.
```

## 2. Short Verse Reference

Mention a verse briefly and explain it.

Example:

```text
Proverbs says that a gentle answer turns away wrath. That does not mean weakness. It means strength under control.
```

## 3. Story-Based

Use a biblical story as a mirror.

Example:

```text
Joseph had every reason to become bitter. But his story shows that betrayal does not have to become your identity.
```

## 4. Application-Based

Connect the principle to a choice.

Example:

```text
Forgiveness begins when you stop feeding the wound with revenge.
```

Avoid:

- quoting too much
- stacking verses
- using verses without explanation
- using Scripture as decoration
- forcing a verse into every paragraph
- making promises Scripture does not make

# Default Script Structure

## 1. Opening Hook

Purpose:

Capture the viewer through emotional recognition.

The hook should start with pain, tension, or a surprising reframe.

Good opening patterns:

```text
Sometimes the thing you are carrying is not what happened to you.
It is what you refuse to release.
```

```text
You can be right and still lose your peace.
```

```text
Not every open door is from God.
```

```text
Pride does not always shout.
Sometimes it simply refuses to say sorry.
```

```text
Faith does not always show you the whole road.
Sometimes it gives enough light for the next step.
```

Avoid starting with:

```text
Today we are going to talk about forgiveness.
```

```text
In this video I will explain three biblical principles.
```

```text
The Bible is very important.
```

The opening should create emotional gravity immediately.

---

## 2. Recognition

Purpose:

Make the viewer feel seen.

Describe the human struggle in concrete terms.

Example:

```text
You replay the conversation again.
You remember the words.
The tone.
The moment they crossed the line.
And even though the person is not in the room anymore, something inside you keeps going back there.
```

Recognition should be specific.

Avoid generic statements like:

```text
Many people struggle with forgiveness.
```

Instead, show what it feels like.

---

## 3. Wrong Response

Purpose:

Show the natural but unwise reaction.

The wrong response should feel understandable.

Example:

```text
So you hold on.
Not because you want to be bitter.
But because letting go feels like saying it did not matter.
```

The viewer should not feel mocked.

They should feel understood.

---

## 4. Consequence

Purpose:

Show what the wrong response does over time.

Example:

```text
But bitterness does something quiet.
It does not only remember the wound.
It gives the wound a place to live.
```

The consequence should create tension.

Use visual language.

Examples:

- the chain gets heavier
- the wall gets taller
- the fire spreads
- the mirror distorts
- the burden bends the back
- the path gets darker

---

## 5. Biblical Principle

Purpose:

Introduce light.

The biblical principle should feel like clarity entering the struggle.

Example:

```text
This is why biblical forgiveness is so different from pretending.
The Bible does not ask you to call evil good.
It calls you to stop letting evil keep ruling your heart.
```

The principle should correct a misunderstanding.

Good principle moves:

- forgiveness is not pretending
- patience is not passivity
- humility is not humiliation
- repentance is not rejection
- wisdom is not fear
- peace is not weakness
- obedience is not prison
- faith is not pretending fear does not exist

---

## 6. Visual Metaphor Expansion

Purpose:

Make the principle memorable.

The script should naturally support visual metaphors.

Example:

```text
Unforgiveness is like a chain.
At first, you think you are holding it.
But slowly, it starts holding you.
```

The metaphor should be simple enough for the stickman to act out.

Do not overload with multiple metaphors in the same section.

---

## 7. Turning Point

Purpose:

Show the first small wise choice.

The turning point should not feel unrealistic.

Example:

```text
The first step is not pretending you are fine.
The first step is telling the truth:
This hurt me.
But I will not let hatred become my home.
```

The turning point should be actionable.

---

## 8. Practical Application

Purpose:

Give the viewer something to do.

The application should be simple and specific.

Examples:

- pause before answering
- pray honestly before reacting
- apologize first
- forgive without denying pain
- release one replayed memory
- tell the truth before the lie grows
- take one wise step
- lower one brick in the wall
- choose gratitude for one thing
- ask for help
- return instead of hiding

Avoid vague applications:

```text
Just trust God.
Be better.
Have more faith.
Stop being angry.
```

---

## 9. Hopeful Close

Purpose:

Leave the viewer with light.

The close should be emotionally satisfying.

Example:

```text
You may not be able to change what happened.
But with God’s help, you can choose what it becomes inside you.
The chain can be dropped.
The wound does not have to become your home.
```

End with hope, not shame.

```text
450–900 words
```

Best for:

- 3–6 minute videos
- one topic
- one metaphor
- one clear application

Length:

```text
1,200–2,000 words
```

Best for:

- 8–12 minute videos
- one main principle
- deeper emotional build
- several examples

## Long Script

Length:

```text

```

Best for:

- 15–22 minute videos
- biblical story retelling
- multiple emotional chapters
- one main motif with several callbacks
- deeper narrative retention

# Pacing Rules

The pacing should feel spoken, not written.

Use:

- short emotional lines
- pauses
- repetition with purpose
- simple sentence rhythm
- concrete imagery
- gradual escalation
- clear turns

Example:

```text
You hold on.
You replay it.
You tell yourself it is justice.

But sometimes what feels like justice
slowly becomes a prison.
```

Avoid:

- long paragraphs
- dense theological exposition
- too many ideas at once
- abstract explanations without images
- constant verse references
- fast jumps between metaphors

---

# Sentence Rhythm

Use a mix of:

## Short impact lines

```text
That is the trap.
```

```text
The wound stays alive.
```

```text
The wall gets taller.
```

```text
The fire spreads.
```

## Reflective lines

```text
Because sometimes the thing that feels like protection is actually the thing keeping you trapped.
```

## Gentle reframes

```text
Forgiveness is not saying the pain was small.
It is saying the pain will not become your master.
```

## Hopeful lines

```text
The first step may be small.
But small steps still move you toward light.
```

# Retention Devices

Use retention through meaning, not manipulation.

## 1. Emotional Recognition

The viewer stays because they feel understood.

Example:

```text
You have not said their name in weeks, but your heart still answers when the memory knocks.
```

## 2. Reframe

The viewer stays because the principle is being seen differently.

Example:

```text
Patience is not doing nothing.
Patience is refusing to let panic become your guide.
```

## 3. Visual Motif

The viewer stays to see what happens to the symbol.

Example:

```text
The chain appears.
The chain tightens.
The chain is noticed.
The chain loosens.
The chain drops.
```

## 4. Open Loop

The script introduces a question that resolves later.

Example:

```text
But there is a reason the chain feels so hard to drop.
And it is not because you are weak.
```

## 5. Transformation Promise

The viewer stays because they want the emotional resolution.

Example:

```text
By the end, the question is not whether the wound mattered.
It is whether the wound gets to lead your life.
```

# Visual Writing Rules

The script must be easy for the Visual Planner to convert into scenes.

Use visual language:

- chain
- wall
- fire
- lamp
- burden
- road
- mirror
- door
- bridge
- seed
- storm
- light
- hands
- stone
- path
- window

Avoid invisible abstractions without imagery:

- spiritual optimization
- moral alignment
- psychological integration
- abstract transformation
- theological complexity

When abstract ideas are needed, attach them to a visual.

Example:

Weak:

```text
Pride damages relationships.
```

Strong:

```text
Pride builds a wall one brick at a time, until the person you love is standing on the other side.
```

# Biblical Story Writing Rules

When using a biblical story:

- keep the story simple
- focus on the emotional lesson
- do not overload historical details
- do not turn the story into pure self-help
- connect the story to the viewer’s life
- preserve the biblical meaning
- use the stickman as a modern emotional mirror if needed

Structure:

```text
1. Viewer pain
2. Biblical story introduced as mirror
3. Key emotional moment in the story
4. Principle revealed
5. Modern application
6. Hopeful close
```

Example:

```text
Joseph’s story is not only about betrayal.
It is about what happens when betrayal does not get the final word over your heart.
```

# Script Quality Checklist

Before approving a script, check:

- Does it start with emotional recognition?
- Does it avoid a generic intro?
- Is the human pain specific?
- Is the wrong response understandable?
- Is the biblical principle clear?
- Is the principle faithful and not overpromised?
- Is there one strong visual motif?
- Can the main stickman act out the script visually?
- Does the script move from pain to clarity to hope?
- Is the tone compassionate?
- Is there a practical application?
- Does the close bring light?
- Does it avoid prosperity framing?
- Does it avoid shame-heavy manipulation?
- Does it avoid denominational or political arguments?
- Does it sound spoken rather than written?

If several answers are no, rewrite the script.

---

# Script Editing Rules

When editing a script, preserve:

- the opening hook if it works
- the main emotional pain
- the core biblical principle
- the main visual metaphor
- the transformation arc
- the hopeful close

Cut first:

- repeated ideas
- abstract explanations
- generic religious filler
- unnecessary verse stacking
- long transitions
- paragraphs that repeat the same point
- motivational clichés
- excessive disclaimers
- anything not visually stageable

---

# Final Rule

A Christian Life script should not merely explain what is right.

It should help the viewer see:

```text
This is what I am carrying.
This is how I have been responding.
This is what biblical wisdom reveals.
This is the small step I can take.
There is still light for this part of my life.
```
