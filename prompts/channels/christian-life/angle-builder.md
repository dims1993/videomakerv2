# Christian Life — Angle Builder

## Purpose

This file defines how to transform a broad biblical topic into a strong video angle for the Christian Life channel.

The Angle Builder exists to prevent generic biblical videos.

A topic is not enough.

A strong video needs:

**TOPIC → HUMAN PAIN → BIBLICAL PRINCIPLE → EMOTIONAL PROMISE → VISUAL METAPHOR → PRACTICAL TRANSFORMATION**

The goal is to create video angles that are simple, emotional, visual, biblically grounded, and useful for real life.

---

## Core Function

The Angle Builder should take a broad topic such as:

- forgiveness
- pride
- patience
- fear
- anger
- anxiety
- faith
- gratitude
- wisdom
- temptation
- humility
- loneliness
- obedience
- comparison
- guilt
- repentance
- trust
- prayer
- perseverance

And turn it into a focused video idea with:

- a specific emotional struggle
- a clear biblical principle
- a strong visual metaphor
- a human story arc
- a practical life lesson
- a retention-friendly promise

---

## Default Angle Formula

Use this formula:

```text id="7kg61f"
When someone feels {emotional_pain}, they usually try to solve it through {wrong_response}. But biblical wisdom reveals that {biblical_principle}. The video shows this through {visual_metaphor}, moving the viewer from {starting_identity} to {transformed_identity}.
```

---

## Example

Topic:

```text id="6ri10m"
Forgiveness
```

Weak angle:

```text id="mzrtgx"
Why forgiveness is important.
```

Strong angle:

```text id="gnvad6"

The video shows unforgiveness as a heavy chain connected to a dark stone, moving the viewer from “the person still carrying the wound” to “the person learning to release what is controlling them.”
```

# Field Definitions

## Topic

The broad biblical or life topic.

Examples:

- forgiveness
- pride
- anger
- patience
- fear
- anxiety
- gratitude
- humility
- temptation
- comparison
- loneliness
- wisdom
- obedience
- prayer
- repentance

---

## Biblical Principle

The biblical truth behind the video.

It should be simple, practical, and faithful to biblical wisdom.

Good examples:

- Forgiveness releases the heart from bitterness.
- Humility opens the way to peace.
- Patience protects the soul from reckless choices.
- Wisdom gives light for the next step, not always the whole road.
- Gratitude changes what the heart notices.
- Anger feels powerful at first, but uncontrolled anger destroys peace.
- Faith continues walking even when the full path is not visible.
- Repentance is not humiliation; it is returning to life.
- Obedience can feel narrow before it feels freeing.
- Prayer turns the heart back toward God before it tries to fix everything alone.

Avoid principles that are:

- too vague
- too abstract
- too academic
- too prosperity-focused
- too shame-based
- too simplistic

Bad examples:

- Be good.
- God wants you happy.
- Just have faith.
- Never be afraid.
- Forgive and forget.
- If you pray, everything will be easy.

---

## Human Pain

The emotional problem the viewer recognizes immediately.

Examples:

- feeling betrayed
- carrying resentment
- feeling unseen
- being tired of waiting
- feeling guilty
- feeling spiritually dry
- comparing your life to others
- being angry but not knowing why
- wanting control
- being afraid of the future
- feeling abandoned
- wanting revenge
- feeling stuck
- feeling ashamed
- feeling lost
- not knowing what decision to make

The pain should feel concrete, not abstract.

Weak:

```text id="ydveem"
People struggle with sin.
```

Strong:

```text id="t2eexx"
You keep replaying what someone did to you, and every time you remember it, the wound feels alive again.
```

---

## Wrong Response

The common human way of dealing with the pain before biblical wisdom enters.

Examples:

- holding a grudge
- pretending everything is fine
- reacting in anger
- building emotional walls
- chasing approval
- comparing yourself constantly
- trying to control everything
- lying to avoid consequences
- avoiding prayer
- giving up too soon
- rushing ahead without wisdom
- blaming everyone else
- isolating yourself
- hiding guilt
- seeking revenge
- numbing the pain with distraction

The wrong response should be understandable.

The viewer should think:

**“I have done that.”**

---

## Emotional Hook

The hook is the first emotional idea of the video.

It should be specific, direct, and human.

A good hook creates recognition before explanation.

Examples:

```text id="9pqg5z"
The thing you refuse to forgive may be the thing still controlling you.
```

```text id="u4i5au"
You can be right and still lose your peace.
```

```text id="8d673f"
Not every open door is from God.
```

```text id="qokp2k"
Sometimes pride does not look loud. Sometimes it looks like refusing to say sorry.
```

```text id="rdlk4p"
The Bible does not teach patience because waiting is easy. It teaches patience because waiting reveals the heart.
```

```text id="rc0py8"
Some burdens were never meant to be carried alone.
```

---

## Core Promise

The core promise tells the viewer why the video is worth watching.

It should answer:

**What will the viewer understand by the end?**

Examples:

- You will understand why forgiveness is not weakness but freedom.
- You will see why anger feels powerful but quietly steals peace.
- You will understand why waiting can shape the heart.
- You will see how pride builds walls before you notice them.
- You will understand why gratitude changes the way you experience life.
- You will see why God’s wisdom often gives enough light for the next step, not the whole road.
- You will understand why repentance is not the end of your story, but the doorway back.

The promise should not overpromise.

Avoid:

- Your life will instantly change.
- God will fix everything today.
- This verse will make you successful.
- You will never struggle again.

---

## Visual Metaphor

The visual metaphor turns the biblical principle into an image.

This is essential for the channel.

Examples:

| Topic          | Visual Metaphor                       |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Forgiveness    | chain attached to a dark stone        |
| Pride          | wall built brick by brick             |
| Anger          | small fire growing out of control     |
| Wisdom         | small lamp lighting one step          |
| Faith          | walking through darkness with a lamp  |
| Guilt          | heavy backpack                        |
| Repentance     | open door with light                  |
| Patience       | seed slowly growing roots             |
| Comparison     | distorted mirror                      |
| Anxiety        | storm cloud above the stickman        |
| Prayer         | opening a window toward light         |
| Obedience      | narrow path toward peace              |
| Temptation     | shortcut sign beside a dangerous path |
| Gratitude      | empty hands becoming open hands       |
| Reconciliation | broken bridge being repaired          |

The metaphor must be:

- simple
- emotionally clear
- easy to animate
- easy to understand in less than two seconds
- connected to the life problem
- connected to the biblical principle

---

## Main Character Situation

This is the concrete scene where the main stickman experiences the angle.

Examples:

- the stickman carries a heavy chain connected to a stone
- the stickman builds a wall between himself and another stickman
- the stickman stands at a crossroads between a wide dark road and a narrow lit path
- the stickman holds a small lamp in darkness
- the stickman looks into a cracked mirror
- the stickman tries to hold back a growing fire
- the stickman kneels beside a heavy burden
- the stickman reaches toward an open door with light behind it
- the stickman waters a tiny seed
- the stickman repairs a broken bridge

The main character situation should be the visual heart of the video.

---

## Emotional Transformation

The transformation defines the emotional arc of the viewer.

Use this structure:

```text id="o4jzfw"
from {painful_state} to {biblical_clarity_state}
```

Examples:

- from bitterness to release
- from pride to humility
- from fear to trust
- from guilt to repentance
- from confusion to wisdom
- from anger to peace
- from comparison to gratitude
- from loneliness to being seen
- from control to surrender
- from impatience to faithful waiting
- from shame to returning
- from despair to hope

---

## Practical Application

The practical application is the action the viewer can take.

It should be simple and realistic.

Examples:

- forgive without pretending the wound did not matter
- pause before answering in anger
- apologize first
- pray before reacting
- tell the truth before the lie grows
- choose gratitude for one specific thing today
- take one wise step instead of demanding the whole road
- stop feeding comparison
- lower one brick in the wall you built
- ask for help instead of carrying the burden alone
- return to God instead of hiding in shame
- choose the narrow right path even when the shortcut looks easier

Avoid vague applications:

- be better
- trust more
- have faith
- stop sinning
- be positive

---

## Viewer Identity Shift

This defines how the viewer should see themselves differently by the end.

Examples:

- from victim of the wound to person learning release
- from angry reactor to peacemaker in training
- from someone trapped by guilt to someone invited to return
- from someone lost in comparison to someone learning gratitude
- from someone demanding control to someone learning trust
- from someone afraid of the dark to someone willing to follow the next lighted step
- from someone building walls to someone choosing humility
- from someone hiding from God to someone walking back toward Him

Identity shift is important for retention because the viewer is not just learning an idea.

They are imagining a different version of themselves.

## Retention Mechanism

Each angle should have a built-in retention engine.

Possible mechanisms:

## 1. Hidden Cost

The viewer stays to understand the unseen damage of a common behavior.

Examples:

- the hidden cost of anger
- the hidden cost of comparison
- the hidden cost of pride
- the hidden cost of refusing to forgive

## 2. Reframe

The viewer stays because the video changes the meaning of a familiar biblical principle.

Examples:

- forgiveness is not weakness
- patience is not passivity
- humility is not humiliation
- repentance is not the end
- obedience is not restriction

## 3. Emotional Mirror

The viewer stays because the video describes something they have felt but not named.

Examples:

- replaying a wound
- feeling unseen
- being tired of waiting
- feeling too ashamed to return
- pretending to be fine

## 4. Visual Mystery

The viewer stays because a visual metaphor is introduced and resolved.

Examples:

- Why is the stickman carrying a chain?
- Why is he building a wall?
- Why is the lamp so small?
- Why does the mirror distort him?
- Why does the wide path feel dangerous?

## 5. Transformation Promise

The viewer stays to see the movement from pain to hope.

Examples:

- from bitterness to release
- from fear to trust
- from guilt to return
- from pride to peace
- from confusion to wisdom

---

# Angle Quality Checklist

Before accepting an angle, check:

- Is the human pain specific?
- Is the biblical principle clear?
- Is the visual metaphor strong?
- Can the main stickman act it out?
- Is there emotional tension?
- Is there hope?
- Is the lesson practical?
- Is the title clickable without being manipulative?
- Is the thumbnail visually simple?
- Does the angle avoid generic religious language?
- Does it bring light rather than just guilt?
- Does it respect biblical meaning?
- Can the viewer recognize themselves in it?

If the answer is no to several of these, rebuild the angle.

# Angle Strength Levels

## Weak Angle

A weak angle is broad and generic.

Example:

```text id="77ovig"
Forgiveness is important.
```

Problem:

- no human pain
- no tension
- no visual metaphor
- no emotional promise
- no transformation

---

## Good Angle

A good angle has a clear principle and practical value.

Example:

```text id="ewmj8g"
Forgiveness helps you stop carrying bitterness.
```

Better, but still simple.

---

## Strong Angle

A strong angle has pain, metaphor, tension, and transformation.

Example:

```text id="2avru5"
The person who hurt you may no longer be in the room, but bitterness can keep dragging their shadow behind you. This video shows unforgiveness as a chain connected to a dark stone, revealing why biblical forgiveness is not pretending the wound did not matter, but refusing to let the wound keep owning your future.
```

This is the target.

# Prompt For Generating Angles

Use this prompt to generate angles from a topic.

```text id="85su96"
You are the Angle Builder for the Christian Life YouTube channel.

The channel creates illustrated storytelling videos using a recurring emotional stickman character to make biblical principles simple, visual, emotional, and useful for real life.

Your task is to transform the topic into 5 strong video angles.

Each angle must include:

- Topic
- Biblical principle
- Human pain
- Wrong response
- Emotional hook
- Core promise
- Visual metaphor
- Main character situation
- Emotional transformation
- Practical application
- Viewer identity shift
- Suggested title
- Suggested thumbnail concept
- Suggested opening line
- Retention mechanism
- Avoid

Rules:

- Make the angle emotionally specific.
- Make the biblical principle practical and faithful to biblical wisdom.
- Avoid generic religious language.
- Avoid prosperity gospel promises.
- Avoid fear-based manipulation.
- Avoid denominational disputes.
- Use simple visual metaphors that can be acted out by a stickman.
- The main stickman should appear by default.
- Each angle should move from pain to clarity to hope.
- The viewer should feel seen, gently challenged, and encouraged.
- Keep the language simple, cinematic, and useful for YouTube storytelling.

Topic: {topic}
```

Forgiveness.

Hidden cost + visual mystery + transformation promise.

# Final Rule

A strong Christian Life angle should never be only:

```text id="d7u4zi"
This is what the Bible says about X.
```

It should be:

```text id="tl6mi1"
This is the pain you recognize, the wrong way you try to carry it, the biblical wisdom that brings light, and the simple choice that can begin to change your life.
```
