# Script Writer

## 1. Scope

This writing system is inferred from two unique supplied transcripts:

- a long list video built around **33 habits** for doing hard things;
- a more developed list video built around **7 subtle morning habits**.

The two scripts share a recognizable framework even though their section lengths differ.

This file captures the underlying structure rather than copying sentences.

---

## 2. Channel promise

The recurring content promise is:

```text
practical psychology
+
simple behavior change
+
low-friction actions
+
science-aware framing
+
no extreme self-optimization
```

The narrator repeatedly positions the advice against:

- relying on motivation;
- overcomplicated productivity systems;
- extreme routines;
- instant gratification;
- avoidant behavior;
- popular but oversimplified concepts.

---

## 3. Voice

### Default voice

```text
calm
direct
practical
non-judgmental
conversational
specific
slightly coaching
```

### Sentence behavior

The writing frequently uses:

- direct second person: "you";
- short declarative lines after longer explanation;
- rhetorical questions;
- concrete everyday examples;
- contrast pairs;
- repetition for emphasis;
- "not X, Y" reframing;
- simple commands.

Example structural pattern:

```text
Not finishing.
Not doing an amazing job.
Just ten minutes.
```

The exact wording should change, but the cadence is useful.

---

## 4. Core script formula

The most reusable unit is:

```text
HABIT TITLE
↓
RELATABLE SCENARIO
↓
PROBLEM / MISCONCEPTION
↓
BEHAVIORAL MECHANISM
↓
SIMPLE REFRAME
↓
CONCRETE ACTION
↓
WHY IT HELPS
↓
BRIDGE TO NEXT HABIT
```

Not every section needs every block, but the order is highly reusable.

---

## 5. Hook formula

The stronger transcript opens with a concrete near-future scenario rather than abstract advice.

Template:

```text
Tomorrow [specific moment], notice [automatic behavior].
You probably do it before you even think about it.
That small behavior reveals [larger mechanism].
In this video, you'll learn [number] subtle habits that [clear benefit].
```

Alternative hook:

```text
Your brain gets better at whatever you repeatedly ask it to do — including [undesired pattern].
But the opposite can be trained too.
These [number] habits will help you [benefits] without [extreme requirement].
```

### Hook requirements

Within the opening:
- create self-recognition;
- establish a mechanism;
- promise practical actions;
- reject an extreme framing.

---

## 6. Habit title formula

Strong titles are behavioral instructions.

Examples of title shapes:

```text
Stop [automatic mistake]
Make [desired action] ridiculously clear
Use [small tool]
Move [distraction] out of reach
Protect [valuable resource]
Give [future self] one gift
Practice [small behavior]
Collect [identity evidence]
```

Avoid vague titles such as:

```text
Be productive
Improve discipline
Focus more
```

A title should imply a visible action.

---

## 7. Relatable scenario block

Start many sections with an ordinary moment.

Template:

```text
Imagine [specific everyday situation].
You [initial action].
Then [small obstacle / thought / temptation].
A few seconds later, [undesired behavior].
```

Good domains:

- desk work;
- studying;
- exercise;
- mornings;
- phone use;
- cleaning;
- email;
- writing;
- sleep;
- waiting;
- commuting;
- household friction.

The example should be easy to visualize.

---

## 8. Contrast engine

The scripts frequently teach through contrasts.

### Pattern A — vague vs concrete

```text
"work on project"
vs
"open the file and write the first heading"
```

### Pattern B — motivation vs action

```text
"do I feel like it?"
vs
"what do I do next?"
```

### Pattern C — immediate reward vs delayed reward

```text
scroll then work
vs
work then scroll
```

### Pattern D — intensity vs reliability

```text
huge comeback day
vs
small repeated effort
```

### Pattern E — rest vs avoidance

```text
bounded intentional rest
vs
open-ended escape
```

### Pattern F — abstract identity vs behavioral evidence

```text
"I want to be disciplined"
vs
"I repeatedly did the disciplined action"
```

Use at least one strong contrast per major section when natural.

---

## 9. Mechanism explanation

The scripts often include a compact psychological explanation.

The preferred style is:

```text
behavior
→ consequence
→ what the brain learns
```

Example template:

```text
Every time [behavior] is followed by [immediate consequence], your brain gets another repetition of that sequence. Over time, [response] becomes more familiar and easier to repeat.
```

Avoid sounding like an academic paper.

Science should explain the practical rule, not take over the section.

---

## 10. Research language

The transcripts use cautious research framing.

Useful phrasing:

```text
research suggests...
experimental evidence has found...
reviews support a relationship between...
psychologists often describe...
behavioral economists call this...
```

Do not overclaim causality.

When producing a final public script, factual research claims should be independently sourced and verified.

---

## 11. Reframe block

A common move is to change the question.

Pattern:

```text
Don't ask:
[emotion-based / vague question]

Ask:
[behavior-based / concrete question]
```

Examples of functional transformations:

```text
motivation → next action
discipline → environment design
perfect session → minimum version
guilt → next useful action
productivity system → friction reduction
```

---

## 12. Action block

Every habit should end with something the viewer can do.

A strong action has:

```text
cue
+
physical behavior
+
small boundary
```

Template:

```text
Tomorrow, when [cue], do [specific physical action] for [small amount / until clear stopping point].
```

Bad:

```text
be more intentional
```

Good:

```text
before opening social media, write one sentence describing the most important thing you want from the day
```

---

## 13. Smallness principle

The scripts repeatedly reduce intimidating actions.

Common reducers:

```text
one minute
ten minutes
one task
one question
one physical action
one small problem
one hard thing
one week
```

This creates a recognizable channel philosophy:

> behavior should be small enough to repeat, but meaningful enough to train the desired response.

---

## 14. Section bridge

The 7-habit script uses explicit bridges to create momentum.

Bridge formula:

```text
And that leads to the next habit, because [connection between current mechanism and next mechanism].
```

Alternative:

```text
Once that changes, something else becomes easier...
```

Use bridges in shorter list videos where each section is longer.

For very long 30+ item videos, shorter transitions are better to prevent bloat.

---

## 15. Two script modes

### MODE A — Long list / high count

Suitable for:
```text
20–40 habits
```

Section length:
```text
compact
```

Each item focuses on one useful behavior.

Recommended per habit:

```text
80–170 spoken words
```

Typical structure:

```text
title
scenario
reframe
action
1 short why
```

### MODE B — Short list / deep explanation

Suitable for:
```text
5–10 habits
```

Section length:
```text
expanded
```

Recommended per habit:

```text
250–450 spoken words
```

Typical structure:

```text
title
scenario
mechanism
second example
reframe
action
identity/long-term implication
bridge
```

---

## 16. Identity layer

A notable pattern in the morning-habits transcript is moving from behavior to identity.

Structure:

```text
small action
→ repetition
→ evidence
→ expectation
→ identity
```

Use identity framing near the end of the video, not in every section.

It gives the final habits greater emotional weight.

---

## 17. Anti-extreme positioning

The channel often reassures viewers that the method does not require heroic discipline.

Useful structural moves:

```text
You don't need to...
This isn't about...
The goal isn't...
You aren't trying to...
```

Then replace with:

```text
Instead...
The useful question is...
What matters is...
```

This lowers resistance and makes advice feel practical.

---

## 18. Ending formula

### Step 1 — Synthesize

Summarize the common mechanism behind the list.

```text
These habits all train [one underlying behavior].
```

### Step 2 — Reduce expectations

```text
They won't transform everything overnight.
```

### Step 3 — State long-term payoff

```text
Repeated often enough, they change what your brain expects from you.
```

### Step 4 — Immediate implementation

Give the viewer one action before they leave.

```text
Pick one behavior.
Do the first physical step now.
```

### Step 5 — CTA

CTA should come after value delivery.

Typical functions:

```text
like if useful
subscribe for practical science-backed behavior content
return to action rather than binge-watch
```

## 20. Script generator workflow

### Phase 1 — Thesis

Write one sentence:

```text
The viewer thinks X, but the useful behavior is Y because Z.
```

### Phase 2 — Habit inventory

Generate candidate habits.

Each habit must:
- be behaviorally distinct;
- have a physical action;
- avoid overlap;
- fit the thesis.

### Phase 3 — Sequence

Order from:

```text
easy awareness
→ environment
→ action
→ discomfort tolerance
→ consistency
→ identity
→ immediate challenge
```

### Phase 4 — Examples

Give each habit one unique everyday scenario.

Avoid reusing "phone at desk" in every section.

### Phase 5 — Mechanisms

Only include a mechanism when it adds understanding.

### Phase 6 — Bridges

Add bridges selectively.

### Phase 7 — Ending

Unify all habits into one behavioral principle and end with an immediate action.

## 22. Writing QA

For every habit:

- [ ] Is the title behavioral?
- [ ] Is there a concrete scenario?
- [ ] Is the problem familiar?
- [ ] Is the mechanism understandable?
- [ ] Is there one clear reframe?
- [ ] Is there a physical action?
- [ ] Is the recommendation small enough to repeat?
- [ ] Is it distinct from other habits?
- [ ] Is jargon minimized?
- [ ] Does the section naturally produce visual scenes?

For the full script:

- [ ] Does hook create recognition immediately?
- [ ] Does the video promise a specific outcome?
- [ ] Are sections sequenced logically?
- [ ] Are examples varied?
- [ ] Does research support rather than dominate?
- [ ] Is there a unifying thesis?
- [ ] Does the ending return the viewer to action?
