# Christian Life — Project Bible

## Purpose

This file defines the creative, narrative, visual, and emotional direction of the Christian Life channel.

The channel creates illustrated storytelling videos that make biblical teachings easier to understand, remember, and apply in daily life.

The goal is not only to explain Bible principles, but to help viewers feel why those principles matter.

The visual system is simple:

**MAIN STICKMAN + BIBLICAL PRINCIPLE + LIFE SCENARIO**

The main stickman acts as the emotional anchor of the channel.

Through simple scenes, symbolic situations, and everyday life problems, the viewer should understand the practical value of biblical wisdom.

The channel exists to bring light, clarity, comfort, correction, and hope to people who need guidance.

---

## Core Mission

The mission of the channel is:

**To make biblical principles simple, emotional, visual, and useful for real life.**

Each video should help the viewer:

- understand a biblical principle
- see how it applies to daily life
- recognize themselves in the situation
- feel emotionally connected to the lesson
- leave with hope, clarity, or conviction
- take one practical step toward a better life

The channel should not feel like a cold theology lecture.

It should feel like a simple illustrated story that quietly reaches the heart.

---

## Channel Promise

The promise of the channel:

**Biblical wisdom explained through simple visual stories for real life.**

The viewer should feel:

- “I understand this now.”
- “This applies to me.”
- “I needed to hear this.”
- “There is still hope.”
- “God’s principles are practical, not abstract.”
- “The Bible speaks to my actual life.”

The channel is for people who are:

- carrying guilt, fear, anxiety, anger, loneliness, or confusion
- looking for meaning

## Emotional Positioning

The channel should appeal strongly to emotion.

The emotional strategy is:

**pain → recognition → biblical clarity → hope → practical action**

A good video should make the viewer feel:

1. seen
2. understood
3. gently challenged
4. comforted
5. guided
6. hopeful

The channel should not manipulate emotion cheaply.

It should use emotion to help the viewer pay attention to truth.

## Main Creative Formula

Every video should be built around this structure:

**A real human problem + a biblical principle + a simple visual story + a practical life lesson**

Example:

- Problem: someone feels betrayed
- Biblical principle: forgiveness frees the heart
- Visual story: the stickman carries a heavy chain connected to the person who hurt him
- Lesson: forgiveness is not saying the wound did not matter; it is refusing to let the wound own your future

## Narrative Structure

A strong video should usually follow this structure:

## 1. Emotional Hook

Start with a real feeling or situation.

Examples:

- “You can forgive someone and still remember what they did.”
- “Sometimes the heaviest thing you carry is not what happened to you. It is what you refuse to release.”
- “Pride does not always look loud. Sometimes it looks like refusing to say sorry.”
- “You can win an argument and still lose peace.”
- “The Bible does not call patience weakness. It calls it strength under control.”

The hook should create recognition.

The viewer should feel:
**“That sounds like me.”**

---

## 2. Human Scenario

Show the main stickman in a relatable situation.

Examples:

- holding a grudge
- reacting in anger
- comparing himself to others
- ignoring wise advice
- feeling alone
- carrying guilt
- chasing approval
- refusing to forgive
- standing at a moral crossroads
- choosing between easy and right

The scenario should be visual and emotionally readable.

---

## 3. Tension

Make the problem clear.

The video should show what happens when the person lives against the principle.

Examples:

- anger grows heavier
- comparison steals peace
- pride creates distance
- dishonesty creates fear
- impatience causes damage
- bitterness becomes a prison
- selfishness isolates the heart

Tension should be emotional, not sensational.

---

## 4. Biblical Principle

Introduce the biblical wisdom simply.

The principle should be explained in practical language.

Example:

Instead of only saying:

“Forgive others.”

Say:

“The Bible teaches forgiveness not because the wound was small, but because carrying hatred gives the wound a second life.”

The biblical principle should feel alive and relevant.

---

## 5. Visual Metaphor

Use a simple visual metaphor to make the principle memorable.

Examples:

- unforgiveness as a chain
- pride as a wall
- patience as a bridge
- wisdom as a lamp
- anger as fire
- guilt as a heavy bag
- comparison as a mirror that distorts
- prayer as opening a window
- faith as walking with a small lamp in the dark
- obedience as staying on a narrow path
- generosity as an open hand

The metaphor should be easy to understand instantly.

---

## 6. Turning Point

Show the moment the main stickman understands the principle.

This can be visualized as:

- dropping a heavy object
- opening a locked door
- choosing a narrow path
- turning away from a destructive option
- accepting help
- kneeling in prayer
- letting go of a chain
- lighting a lamp
- rebuilding a broken bridge
- choosing silence instead of anger
- extending a hand

The turning point should feel emotionally satisfying.

---

## 7. Practical Application

End with a clear life action.

Examples:

- apologize first
- forgive without pretending it did not hurt
- pause before answering in anger
- stop comparing your path to someone else’s
- tell the truth before the lie grows
- pray before reacting
- choose peace over pride
- practice gratitude daily
- serve someone quietly
- release the need to control everything

The viewer should leave knowing what to do.

---

## 8. Hopeful Close

End with hope, not shame.

The close should remind the viewer that change is possible.

Examples:

- “You are not too late to choose peace.”
- “The chain can be dropped.”
- “The door is not as locked as fear told you.”
- “God’s wisdom does not only correct you. It protects you.”
- “The light may be small at first, but it is still light.”

## Bible Principle Treatment

Biblical principles should be presented with respect, simplicity, and usefulness.

When referencing Scripture:

- keep it understandable
- avoid overloading the viewer
- explain the meaning
- connect it to daily life
- avoid using verses as decoration
- do not force a verse into every scene
- make the principle visual and practical

The channel should respect biblical meaning and avoid twisting verses just to fit a motivational message.

---

## Scripture Usage Rules

Use Scripture as the foundation, not as decoration.

Good usage:

- introduce a principle
- explain the meaning in plain language
- connect the verse to the emotional problem
- show how it applies in a real decision
- end with a practical lesson

Avoid:

- quoting too many verses at once
- using verses without context
- using Scripture only for aesthetic effect
- turning every verse into generic self-help
- promising outcomes the Bible does not promise
- using fear or shame as the main persuasion tool
