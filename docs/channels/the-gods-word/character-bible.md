# TheGodsWord Character / Visual Archetype Bible

## 1. Purpose

TheGodsWord does not use a recurring on-screen presenter, host, mascot, or avatar.

This file defines recurring visual archetypes only.

The goal is to keep human figures, biblical characters, symbolic people, and repeated visual motifs consistent across the channel without turning them into a fixed presenter.

The Visual Planner should use this bible when creating `avatar` scenes and whenever a scene includes people, hands, crowds, biblical figures, or symbolic human presence.

## 2. No Recurring Presenter

There is no modern host character.

Do not create:

- a YouTube presenter,
- a recurring narrator avatar,
- a modern pastor at a desk,
- a studio setup,
- a talking-head figure,
- a branded mascot,
- or a repeated main character who represents the channel.

The narration voice is off-screen.

Images should feel like reflective biblical illustrations, not presenter-led content.

## 3. General Human Figure Style

All human figures should match the channel's visual identity:

- hand-painted watercolor and ink illustration,
- simple biblical clothing,
- soft sketch outlines,
- muted earth tones,
- minimal facial detail,
- humble posture,
- calm body language,
- reverent emotional tone,
- no photorealism,
- no celebrity likeness,
- no exaggerated facial expressions.

Human figures should often be slightly simplified or semi-symbolic.

They should communicate through posture, gesture, silhouette, and placement more than facial detail.

## 4. Jesus Archetype

When Jesus appears, depict Him reverently, simply, and consistently.

Preferred visual traits:

- simple off-white or warm cream robe,
- optional muted beige or light tan outer cloak,
- shoulder-length dark brown hair,
- short natural beard,
- soft facial detail, not hyperrealistic,
- calm compassionate posture,
- gentle hand gestures,
- humble presence,
- warm but subtle light around Him when appropriate.

Jesus should feel:

- reverent,
- compassionate,
- approachable,
- calm,
- spiritually authoritative without theatrical force.

Avoid:

- celebrity likeness,
- photorealistic face,
- glowing superhero depiction,
- fantasy armor,
- aggressive posture,
- exaggerated halo,
- intense magical light beams,
- political symbolism,
- modern clothing,
- mockery,
- casual meme-like depiction.

When possible, Jesus may be shown from a respectful distance, in profile, from behind, or with soft facial detail to avoid over-specific facial realism.

### When to Show Jesus

Use Jesus avatar scenes when the narration's meaning depends on Christ Himself being visibly present, especially when the script emphasizes:

- the prayer of Jesus,
- the humanity of Jesus,
- the sorrow or anguish of Jesus,
- the obedience of Jesus,
- the intercession or priestly role of Jesus,
- Jesus asking, surrendering, rising, walking, teaching, healing, or responding,
- a contrast between Jesus and the viewer,
- a Gospel-centered claim that depends on what Christ did.

Do not overuse Jesus avatar scenes.

But do not avoid Jesus when His visible presence is necessary for the image to carry the meaning.

### Symbolic alternatives when Jesus is not shown directly

If Jesus is not shown directly, the symbolic image must still make Christ's action or presence clear through:

- Gethsemane setting,
- cup,
- robe edge,
- kneeling posture,
- hands lowered in prayer,
- cross silhouette,
- empty place where Christ has stood,
- garden path,
- disciples at a distance,
- or other clear biblical context.

A generic scroll, lamp, olive branch, or cup is not enough when the narration is specifically about Jesus' prayer, humanity, obedience, or suffering.

### Weak vs. better examples

Weak:

`A lamp, olive branch, and cup on parchment representing maturity and reverence.`

Better:

`Jesus kneeling in Gethsemane from a respectful distance, open hands lowered toward the ground, a small cup nearby, showing that this is the honest prayer of the Son, not a weak or immature prayer.`

Weak:

`A symbolic object on parchment representing intercession.`

Better:

`Jesus shown from behind in simple off-white robes, standing between the viewer and a distant cross-shaped shadow, suggesting His priestly role without theatrical spectacle.`

Weak:

`Open hands and a lamp representing surrender.`

Better:

`Jesus bowed low among olive roots, the cup still nearby, with soft dawn light beginning behind Him, showing obedience under pressure rather than generic surrender.`

## 5. Disciples Archetype

Disciples should appear as humble ordinary men from a biblical setting.

Preferred visual traits:

- simple robes in muted browns, tans, grays, dusty blues, or olive tones,
- sandals,
- simple head coverings only when appropriate,
- varied but understated silhouettes,
- beards on some figures,
- attentive posture,
- simple gestures of listening, questioning, following, rowing, or walking.

They should not look like fantasy warriors, kings, or theatrical actors.

The disciples should feel human and ordinary.

Common uses:

- listening to Jesus,
- walking along a road,
- sitting near a field,
- standing in a boat,
- reacting quietly to a teaching,
- looking confused, humbled, afraid, or thoughtful.

## 6. Anonymous Believer Archetype

This archetype represents the viewer spiritually without becoming a fixed character.

Use for scenes about prayer, waiting, doubt, repentance, obedience, surrender, and reflection.

Preferred visual traits:

- simple robe or timeless modest clothing depending on the scene,
- often shown from behind, in silhouette, or with minimal facial detail,
- kneeling, standing at a crossroads, holding a lamp, walking a narrow path, praying, releasing stones, or looking toward light,
- emotionally restrained but clear posture.

This figure should not look like a specific modern influencer, celebrity, or presenter.

It may be male or female depending on the narration, but should usually remain universal and symbolic.

## 7. Farmer / Sower Archetype

Use for parables and agricultural images.

Preferred visual traits:

- humble rural figure,
- simple earth-toned robe,
- cloth satchel with seeds,
- hand scattering seed,
- walking through a field,
- calm practical posture,
- face simplified or partly turned away.

Common uses:

- sowing seed,
- examining soil,
- harvesting wheat,
- standing near weeds,
- holding grain,
- working quietly at sunrise.

The farmer should feel biblical, humble, and symbolic.

## 8. Shepherd Archetype

Use for sheep, guidance, lostness, care, and protection.

Preferred visual traits:

- simple robe and cloak,
- wooden staff,
- quiet posture,
- small group of sheep,
- hillside or wilderness path,
- warm soft light.

The shepherd may sometimes represent biblical care, but do not automatically make every shepherd Jesus unless the scene explicitly requires it.

Common uses:

- searching for a lost sheep,
- guiding sheep along a path,
- standing near a gate,
- carrying a sheep gently,
- watching over a flock at sunrise or dusk.

## 9. Crowd Archetype

Crowds should be used sparingly and kept visually simple.

Preferred approach:

- small groups rather than massive detailed crowds,
- simplified figures,
- soft silhouettes,
- muted robes,
- faces not individually detailed,
- arranged around Jesus or a biblical scene,
- clear focus on the main subject.

Avoid dense, chaotic, hyper-detailed crowd scenes.

The crowd should support the spiritual idea, not distract from it.

## 10. Hands Archetype

Hands are an important recurring visual motif.

Use hands to express spiritual action without needing a full person.

Common hand images:

- hand scattering seed,
- hand receiving bread,
- hand holding a lamp,
- hands opened in prayer,
- hand releasing stones,
- hand reaching toward water,
- hand holding a scroll,
- hand touching soil,
- hand pulling weeds,
- hand knocking at a door,
- hand reaching out in a storm.

Style rules:

- simple watercolor hands,
- natural gestures,
- not photorealistic,
- avoid distorted fingers,
- avoid overly dramatic poses,
- use close compositions with negative space.

Hands should feel humble, human, and symbolic.

## 11. Symbolic Heart / Inner Life Archetype

The channel may use symbolic visualizations of the heart, but they should remain tasteful and metaphorical.

Good approaches:

- soil shaped subtly like a heart,
- roots growing beneath a heart-shaped patch of ground,
- a small lamp glowing inside a simple clay vessel,
- stones being removed from soil,
- weeds choking a young plant,
- dry ground receiving rain,
- a closed door opening to warm light.

Avoid:

- anatomical hearts,
- horror imagery,
- gore,
- intense red drama,
- medical realism,
- cheesy glowing heart graphics.

The inner life should be visualized through biblical metaphors, not modern cliché graphics.

## 12. Biblical Women Archetype

When depicting women from Scripture or symbolic women, use reverent simplicity.

Preferred visual traits:

- modest biblical robes,
- muted warm colors,
- soft head covering when appropriate,
- gentle posture,
- minimal facial detail,
- calm emotional expression,
- active but not theatrical presence.

Common uses:

- Mary sitting at Jesus' feet,
- a woman at a well,
- a woman praying,
- a woman holding a lamp,
- a woman listening,
- a woman walking a quiet path,
- a woman returning or receiving mercy.

Avoid sexualized styling, glamorous fashion, modern influencer appearance, or excessive beauty emphasis.

## 13. Biblical Villain / Opposition Archetype

When depicting opposition, pride, hardness of heart, accusation, or resistance, avoid cartoon villains.

Preferred approach:

- restrained posture,
- turned-away face,
- crossed arms,
- standing outside a warm-lit doorway,
- shadowed but not evil-looking,
- visual distance from mercy, light, or community.

Examples:

- the older brother outside the house,
- accusers holding stones,
- a proud figure standing apart,
- a person refusing to release a burden.

Avoid:

- demonic exaggeration unless the script specifically requires spiritual evil,
- horror aesthetics,
- glowing red eyes,
- monstrous features,
- comic-book villain styling.

Most opposition in this channel should feel like a human heart resisting God, not a fantasy enemy.

## 14. Children Archetype

Children may appear in scenes about trust, humility, welcome, dependence, or innocence.

Preferred visual traits:

- simple biblical clothing,
- gentle posture,
- soft rounded silhouette,
- minimal facial detail,
- warm and respectful depiction.

Avoid overly cute cartoon style or modern commercial illustration.

Children should feel natural and reverent within the biblical visual world.

## 15. Angels and Spiritual Beings

Use angels rarely and with restraint.

Preferred approach:

- soft symbolic presence,
- simple robe,
- muted light,
- minimal detail,
- no fantasy armor unless biblically necessary,
- no exaggerated wings unless explicitly appropriate,
- no spectacle.

Avoid:

- fantasy battle angels,
- glowing superhero bodies,
- dramatic magical effects,
- horror demons,
- excessive supernatural imagery.

The channel should usually communicate spiritual truth through Scripture, metaphor, and human response rather than spectacle.

## 16. Emotional Expression

Characters should show emotion through posture and gesture.

Preferred emotional language:

- bowed head,
- open hands,
- turned shoulders,
- quiet stillness,
- reaching outward,
- looking down at soil,
- standing at a threshold,
- sitting alone,
- walking slowly,
- kneeling,
- looking toward light.

Avoid:

- screaming,
- exaggerated crying,
- theatrical despair,
- cartoon reactions,
- aggressive pointing,
- melodramatic poses.

The emotional register should be gentle, serious, and human.

## 17. Clothing Rules

Preferred clothing:

- biblical robes,
- simple cloaks,
- muted fabric,
- earth tones,
- dusty blues,
- olive greens,
- warm beige,
- off-white,
- simple sandals.

Avoid:

- modern suits,
- jeans,
- branded clothing,
- fantasy armor,
- royal costumes unless the biblical story specifically requires royalty,
- glossy fabric,
- exaggerated costume design.

Clothing should support the biblical atmosphere without distracting from the message.

## 18. Face Detail Rules

Faces should usually be simple.

Preferred:

- soft features,
- minimal linework,
- gentle expression,
- slight distance from camera,
- profile view,
- three-quarter view,
- back view,
- face partially in soft light.

Avoid:

- photorealistic portraits,
- over-detailed eyes,
- uncanny expressions,
- celebrity likeness,
- extreme close-up faces,
- AI-looking skin detail.

For Jesus especially, avoid over-specific facial realism.

## 19. Continuity Across Videos

Because there is no fixed cast, continuity comes from archetype consistency.

Across videos, keep consistent:

- biblical clothing style,
- muted watercolor palette,
- simple human proportions,
- reverent posture,
- minimal facial detail,
- symbolic gestures,
- paper texture,
- watercolor and ink treatment.

Do not create a new visual genre for each video.

The audience should recognize the channel's illustrated world even when the characters change.

## 20. Scene Type Guidance

The app supports:

- `avatar`
- `insert`
- `space`

For this channel:

### avatar

Use when a scene includes a human figure or biblical character.

Use `avatar` when a human figure is necessary to communicate the meaning of the narration, not merely when a person is mentioned.

For Christ-centered scenes, `avatar` may be the strongest choice when the point is the action, posture, sorrow, obedience, or presence of Jesus.

But avoid presenter-like, close-up, hyperrealistic, or theatrical Jesus imagery.

Examples:

- Jesus teaching,
- a disciple listening,
- a farmer sowing,
- a person praying,
- Peter reaching from the water,
- the prodigal son walking home,
- the older brother standing outside,
- a shepherd carrying a sheep.

### insert

Use when a scene focuses on an object, hands, metaphor, or symbolic visual.

Examples:

- hand releasing stones,
- seed in soil,
- lamp on a table,
- scroll and bread,
- weeds around wheat,
- roots beneath dry ground,
- open door with warm light.

### space

Use when a scene needs atmosphere, transition, or contemplative silence.

Examples:

- empty road at sunrise,
- wheat field under soft light,
- calm sea,
- desert path,
- olive tree on a hill,
- village in the distance.

## 21. Character Prompt Add-On

When an image includes people, the Visual Planner should add concise character guidance inside the image prompt.

Examples:

For Jesus:

```txt
Jesus depicted reverently in a simple off-white robe, shoulder-length dark brown hair, short natural beard, soft facial detail, calm compassionate posture, gentle hand gesture, not photorealistic
```

For an anonymous believer:

```txt
anonymous reflective believer in simple muted clothing, shown from behind or in soft silhouette, humble posture, minimal facial detail, symbolic and universal
```

For a farmer:

```txt
humble biblical farmer in earth-toned robe, carrying a small seed satchel, hand scattering seed, face simplified, calm posture
```

For a crowd:

```txt
small simplified biblical crowd in muted robes, soft silhouettes, minimal facial detail, arranged quietly around the main subject
```

Do not overload prompts with long character descriptions. Keep them clear and useful.

## 22. What to Avoid

Avoid:

- recurring modern host,
- presenter avatar,
- influencer look,
- celebrity likeness,
- photorealistic portraits,
- uncanny faces,
- over-detailed eyes,
- fantasy costumes,
- superhero anatomy,
- aggressive expressions,
- horror styling,
- sexualized figures,
- glamorous fashion,
- cartoon villain designs,
- chaotic crowds,
- inconsistent clothing styles,
- modern props unless explicitly required.

## 23. Final Principle

TheGodsWord characters are not celebrities, mascots, or entertainment figures.

They are humble visual vessels for biblical truth.

Every person in the image should help the viewer understand the spiritual meaning of the narration with reverence, clarity, and emotional honesty.
