# TheGodsWord Image Prompt Bible

## 1. Visual Identity

TheGodsWord uses a calm, hand-painted biblical illustration style.

The visual world should feel like a reflective Bible study notebook, a devotional watercolor painting, and a simple visual sermon combined.

Images should not look like cinematic film stills, modern stock photography, glossy digital fantasy art, or realistic AI renders.

The core look is:

**hand-painted watercolor and ink Bible study illustration on warm off-white paper.**

Every image should feel:

- reverent,
- calm,
- symbolic,
- readable,
- biblical,
- emotionally gentle,
- visually simple,
- and connected to the narration.

The goal is not to impress with complexity. The goal is to make the spiritual idea visible.

## 2. Core Style Lock

Every image prompt for this channel should include a version of this style lock:

```txt
16:9 horizontal hand-painted watercolor and ink Bible study illustration on warm off-white paper, soft sketch outlines, muted earth tones, wheat gold, olive green, dusty blue shadows, burnt umber soil, gentle parchment light, visible paper texture, loose brush texture, clean symbolic composition, generous negative space, calm reflective mood
```

This style lock should remain consistent across scenes.

The Visual Planner may adapt the subject and composition, but it should not abandon this visual identity.

## 3. Aspect Ratio and Format

All images should be created as:

- 16:9 horizontal
- YouTube video frame composition
- clear at thumbnail and video size
- readable without needing tiny details

Avoid vertical compositions unless explicitly requested.

Avoid square compositions.

Avoid overly complex crowd scenes that become unreadable at video size.

## 4. Overall Look

The preferred look is:

- watercolor wash
- delicate ink linework
- hand-drawn edges
- visible paper grain
- soft atmospheric light
- warm off-white background
- gentle shadows
- simple shapes
- minimal clutter
- one main idea per frame

The image should look illustrated, not photographed.

It should feel human-made, quiet, and devotional.

## 5. Color Palette

Use a restrained biblical watercolor palette.

Preferred colors:

- warm ivory
- parchment beige
- wheat gold
- dusty ochre
- soft brown
- burnt umber
- terracotta
- olive green
- muted sage
- dusty blue
- blue-gray shadow
- soft charcoal ink
- pale sunrise yellow

Avoid:

- neon colors
- saturated fantasy colors
- glossy gradients
- harsh red/black drama
- cyberpunk palettes
- modern corporate blue
- overly bright cartoon colors

The palette should feel ancient, natural, and calm.

## 6. Lighting

Preferred lighting:

- soft sunrise light
- warm parchment glow
- gentle golden hour atmosphere
- diffused light
- quiet interior lamplight
- subtle halo-like warmth when appropriate

Avoid:

- cinematic spotlighting
- harsh horror lighting
- extreme contrast
- dramatic fantasy beams
- overexposed glowing effects
- neon rim light

Light should support reverence and clarity, not spectacle.

## 7. Composition Rules

Each image should communicate one clear visual idea.

Prefer:

- centered or slightly off-center subject
- simple foreground object
- clear silhouette
- wide negative space
- clean background
- symbolic contrast
- gentle visual flow
- readable shapes
- minimal characters

Avoid:

- crowded compositions
- many competing focal points
- tiny important details
- overly cinematic camera angles
- extreme closeups unless emotionally justified
- cluttered backgrounds
- unnecessary props

The viewer should understand the image immediately.

## 8. Visual Language

The channel should use biblical, natural, agricultural, and parable-like imagery.

Strong visual anchors include:

- seeds
- soil
- wheat
- weeds
- thorns
- stones
- narrow roads
- open doors
- lamps
- oil lamps
- scrolls
- clay jars
- bread
- water
- wells
- boats
- nets
- sheep
- shepherd paths
- vineyards
- fig trees
- olive trees
- desert paths
- storm clouds
- calm seas
- hands reaching, receiving, releasing, planting, or praying
- houses built on rock or sand
- fields at sunrise
- simple ancient villages
- crowds listening from a distance

The visuals should often feel like a parable made visible.

## 9. Biblical Environment

Use ancient biblical settings when the scene requires a world:

- Galilean hills
- dusty roads
- wheat fields
- olive groves
- simple stone houses
- desert wilderness
- fishing boats
- shorelines
- village gates
- small courtyards
- simple interiors with lamps and scrolls
- fields, paths, rocks, and trees

Keep environments minimal and symbolic.

Do not overbuild the world.

The image does not need historical-documentary realism. It needs biblical atmosphere, visual clarity, and emotional truth.

## 10. Human Figures

Human figures should be stylized and reverent.

Preferred approach:

- simple biblical robes
- soft facial features
- humble posture
- gentle gestures
- minimal facial detail
- expressive silhouette
- warm human presence
- no celebrity likeness
- no hyperreal faces

Figures can represent:

- Jesus
- disciples
- anonymous believers
- farmers
- shepherds
- crowds
- a praying person
- a waiting person
- a person standing at a crossroads
- a person listening, planting, kneeling, walking, receiving, or returning

Avoid:

- photorealistic faces
- modern fashion unless explicitly needed
- heroic fantasy poses
- muscular superhero anatomy
- exaggerated emotional faces
- uncanny faces
- over-detailed eyes
- aggressive expressions

## 11. Depicting Jesus

When Jesus appears, depict Him reverently and simply.

Preferred approach:

- humble biblical robe
- warm but not exaggerated light
- calm posture
- compassionate presence
- simple facial detail
- gentle gestures
- not overly glamorous
- not photorealistic
- not theatrical

Avoid:

- celebrity likeness
- glowing fantasy superhero depiction
- excessive halo effects
- dramatic action poses
- political symbolism
- mockery
- casual modern styling

Jesus should feel reverent, approachable, and faithful to the tone of Christian reflection.

## 12. Symbolic and Metaphorical Images

Many scenes should be symbolic rather than literal.

Good examples:

- a seed falling into four different types of soil
- a lamp glowing in a dark simple room
- two paths dividing across a parchment landscape
- a heart-shaped patch of soil with roots growing
- a hand releasing stones beside a road
- wheat and weeds growing together
- a small boat in a storm with one figure reaching out
- a door slightly open with warm light inside
- a cracked clay jar being filled with water
- a tree with deep roots beside a dry riverbed

The image should make the narration visible without needing text.

## Drawing From the scriptText

**The assigned scriptText must be the main director of the image prompt.**

Draw the simplest clear image that naturally fits the narration line.

Do not over-engineer the visual.

Do not invent abstract metaphors unless the narration itself is abstract.

When the scriptText names a biblical person, event, place, or action, show it directly and simply.

Do not replace concrete biblical narration with unrelated symbolic objects.

Do not match isolated keywords (for example prayer → scroll, Jesus → cup).

Do not create abstract symbolic puzzles that do not naturally look like the narration.

Avoid vague main subjects such as `symbolic object`, `quiet landscape`, or `biblical atmosphere` unless you name exactly what to draw.

Meaningful does not mean abstract.

Symbolic does not mean indirect.

## Prompt Specificity Rules

The main subject must name what should actually be drawn.

Avoid vague main subjects such as:

- a symbolic object
- simple objects
- contrasting elements
- a teaching diagram
- a quiet landscape
- a biblical scene
- a reflective composition
- a spiritual atmosphere
- a symbolic contrast

unless the prompt names:

- the exact object or figure,
- the setting,
- the spatial relationship,
- the emotional tension,
- and the meaning of the image.

Simple is good. Vague is not.

## Meaning Before Motif

Use channel motifs (cup, scroll, lamp, path, hands, door, seed, soil) only when the scriptText naturally calls for them.

Do not add motifs as generic Christian decoration.

Let the scriptText choose the image first.

## 13. Diagram and Teaching Images

The reference style may include simple explanatory visuals.

Use diagram-like images only when they help clarify the idea.

Acceptable diagram elements:

- simple arrows
- divided soil patches
- before/after comparison
- path comparison
- symbolic scales
- layered roots under soil
- small hand-drawn labels only when explicitly requested

However, text inside generated images should usually be avoided because image models often produce distorted words.

Default rule:

**No text inside the image.**

If labels are needed, the prompt should request only very short hand-lettered labels, but the safer workflow is to add text later during editing.

## 14. Text Policy

Default image prompts must include:

```txt
no text, no captions, no letters, no words
```

Use text inside an image only if explicitly requested.

Do not ask the image model to create Bible verse text on scrolls unless the exact text is required and the workflow accepts possible errors.

Prefer blank scrolls, symbolic scrolls, or space for overlay text.

## 15. Scene Type Interpretation

The app uses these accepted scene types:

- `avatar`
- `insert`
- `space`

For TheGodsWord, interpret them this way:

### avatar

Use for scenes with a human figure or biblical character.

Examples:

- Jesus teaching near a field
- a disciple listening
- a farmer sowing seed
- a person praying alone
- a believer standing on a narrow road
- Peter reaching out in the storm
- the prodigal son returning home

The image is still not a presenter shot. There is no recurring host.

### insert

Use for symbolic objects, metaphors, visual explanations, and close visual ideas.

Examples:

- seed in soil
- lamp on a table
- scroll beside bread
- thorns choking a plant
- roots beneath dry ground
- stones beside a path
- clay jar with water
- open door with light
- divided soil diagram

This will often be the most useful scene type.

### space

Use for quiet landscapes, transitions, atmosphere, or contemplative pauses.

Examples:

- empty road at sunrise
- wheat field in warm light
- calm sea after a storm
- desert path under soft sky
- quiet village in the distance
- olive tree on a hill
- empty shoreline

These images should give the narration room to breathe.

## 16. Image Prompt Structure

A strong image prompt should contain:

1. Style lock
2. Main subject
3. Biblical or symbolic environment
4. Composition
5. Mood
6. Text restriction
7. Negative restrictions

Recommended structure:

```txt
16:9 horizontal hand-painted watercolor and ink Bible study illustration on warm off-white paper, soft sketch outlines, muted earth tones, wheat gold, olive green, dusty blue shadows, gentle parchment light, visible paper texture.

Main subject: [clear visual subject].

Composition: [simple readable composition, one focal point, generous negative space].

Mood: calm, reflective, reverent, emotionally gentle.

No text, no captions, no letters, no words.

Avoid photorealism, 3D render, glossy digital art, neon colors, cinematic realism, clutter, distorted hands, uncanny faces, watermark.
```

## 17. Prompt Writing Rules

Image prompts should be specific, visual, and generative.

Do not write abstract prompts like:

- "show faith"
- "represent obedience"
- "illustrate spiritual growth"
- "make it biblical"
- "show the meaning of the passage"

Instead, convert the idea into visible objects:

- "a small seed sending roots into dark soil"
- "a narrow path crossing a quiet wheat field"
- "a hand releasing a stone beside a dusty road"
- "a lamp glowing beside an open scroll"
- "a person kneeling beside an empty well at sunrise"

The prompt should describe what the image generator should draw.

Additional rules:

- Let the scriptText direct the image.
- Keep prompts concise. Tell the model what to draw, not why the theology matters.
- Do not create decorative religious still lifes unless the scriptText calls for one.
- Do not use scroll, lamp, cup, or olive branch as generic decoration.
- Show biblical scenes directly when the scriptText names them.
- Avoid abstract symbolic puzzles.

## 18. Continuity Rules

Across a full video, the images should feel like they belong to the same illustrated world.

Maintain consistency in:

- paper texture
- watercolor style
- linework
- muted palette
- biblical atmosphere
- gentle lighting
- symbolic simplicity
- 16:9 composition

Avoid sudden changes into:

- photorealism
- anime
- 3D render
- dark fantasy
- modern stock photography
- comic book style
- hyper-polished digital painting

A viewer should feel that the whole video is one coherent illustrated devotional sequence.

## 19. Camera and Framing Language

Use simple framing language.

Preferred:

- wide simple composition
- medium-wide view
- centered symbolic object
- slightly off-center human figure
- close view of hands
- quiet landscape view
- simple side view
- gentle overhead view for diagrams

Avoid:

- ultra-wide cinematic lens
- drone shot
- dramatic low-angle hero shot
- action camera feel
- complex cinematic blocking
- film still language

This channel is not trying to look like a movie. It is trying to look like a reverent illustrated Bible reflection.

## 20. Negative Prompt

Use this negative guidance consistently:

```txt
photorealistic, 3D render, glossy digital art, anime, comic book, neon colors, cyberpunk, modern corporate stock photo, cinematic movie still, heavy contrast, hard shadows, horror, gore, violence, fantasy armor, superhero pose, celebrity likeness, uncanny face, distorted hands, cluttered background, excessive detail, random text, misspelled text, logo, watermark
```

The Visual Planner may adapt the negative prompt if a scene requires special restrictions, but it should never allow the image to become photorealistic, glossy, modern, or cluttered.

## 21. Visual Quality Checklist

Before accepting an image prompt, check:

- Does it follow the assigned scriptText directly and simply?
- Is the main subject concrete and drawable?
- Is it too generic, decorative, or puzzle-like?
- Does it include the style lock, 16:9, no text, and negative restrictions?
- Can the viewer understand it quickly?

If the answer is no, rewrite the prompt.

## 22. Final Principle

The image should never feel like decoration.

Every image should help the viewer understand, feel, or remember the biblical truth being narrated.

The best TheGodsWord image feels like a quiet spiritual insight drawn on paper.
